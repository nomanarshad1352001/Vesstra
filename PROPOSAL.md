# Vesttara — development partnership brief

**Prepared for a demonstration to Vestted Wealth Advisors.** This independent concept is not an endorsed product or a representation of the firm’s actual clients, performance, personnel, or systems.

## What the prototype demonstrates

Vesttara brings portfolio reporting, advisor relationships, a private-client experience, and contextual assistance into one responsive workspace. The presentation uses entirely fictional local data, so the evaluation requires no credentials, custody connections, or sensitive records.

- **Portfolio clarity:** household and practice-level values, performance comparisons, allocation, account drill-down, holdings, transaction filtering, and local CSV exports.
- **Advisor productivity:** a movable five-stage pipeline, contacts, household records, editable tasks and notes, meetings, and activity history. A scripted assistant cites example records and creates follow-up tasks.
- **Client service:** own-household balances, three account types, scoped documents, a sample quarterly PDF, message conversations, and calendar booking.
- **Operational oversight:** illustrative permissions, user directory, audit events, integration diagnostics, and sample API contracts. Client-owned source code, hosting, and administration are explicit delivery objectives.
- **Sellable product quality:** an original navy-and-gold design system, bundled typefaces, responsive navigation, light/dark themes, accessible controls, and a guided product tour.

## Why this fits the development-partner brief

The demo turns broad requirements into workflows an advisory team can test. A task moves from assistant to CRM; a client booking appears in the advisor calendar; role changes preserve session work without exposing other households through the client interface. A locally resettable prototype makes product, usability, and compliance discussions possible before real integration costs begin.

## Proposed delivery: build selectively, reuse responsibly

Build the firm-specific workflows and information architecture. Reuse established React primitives, charting, and open-source tooling rather than recreating infrastructure. AI-assisted scaffolding and test drafting can reduce repetitive work; an engineer reviews and validates each change. The firm defines policy, approves integrations, and owns its accounts.

**Days 1–30:** discovery, approved workflows, role boundaries, integration inventory, and tested design prototype. **Days 31–60:** implementation against approved sandbox providers, verified identity, authorization, and core reporting. **Days 61–90:** acceptance and accessibility testing, security and compliance review, pilot deployment, documentation, and client handoff. Timing is indicative and depends on scope, provider availability, and approvals.

## Scope boundary and next step

This is a product demonstration, not a production RIA system. No trading, investment recommendations, real AI, secure document storage, or regulatory-compliance claims are included. A production engagement would separately scope vendor due diligence, server-enforced access control, retention and audit requirements, operational monitoring, and human review.

**Recommended next step:** a short discovery workshop to prioritize one advisor workflow and one client journey, then agree on acceptance criteria and a fixed-scope pilot.

_Demo only. Dummy data. Not investment advice._
